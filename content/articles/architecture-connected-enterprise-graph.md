Architecture often loses meaning during translation: business intent becomes design, design becomes implementation, and operational constraints emerge along the way.

I find it useful to think of enterprise architecture as a graph. Goals connect to capabilities, applications, data, controls, teams, costs, and risks. Changes ripple through those relationships.

## Follow the connections

A diagram helps explain a particular view. Decisions also need the surrounding context: why a dependency exists, who owns it, and what happens if it changes.

Architects need to move between business conversations and engineering, security, operations, and delivery discussions while preserving that context. Connecting specialists’ perspectives reveals otherwise hidden consequences.

## Keep the graph grounded

Decision records, repositories, and knowledge tools can help us retrieve and challenge those relationships. AI makes this more urgent as it accelerates the production of designs and implementation artifacts.

Our contribution is connecting intent to delivery evidence and challenging assumptions against reality.
