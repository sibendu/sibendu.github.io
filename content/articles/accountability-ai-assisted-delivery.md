Traditional software delivery carries intent through successive handovers. Meaning can be lost as context is reconstructed.

AI accelerates many of those activities: analysis, coding, testing, and documentation. Human attention increasingly goes towards deciding whether the work is right and accepting its consequences.

## Put ownership into the workflow

Accountability needs to be visible before execution begins. A useful workflow makes clear:

- Who defines the intended outcome and acceptable scope.
- Who sets limits on what the AI may do.
- Who decides whether the evidence demonstrates success.
- Who accepts the remaining risk.

Explicit responsibility helps teams challenge plausible but incorrect results.

## Broaden our operating range

My experiments across models, tools, and agent workflows keep bringing me back to judgment. Humans need to connect business intent with design, data, integration, security, and delivery reality.

Effective partnership between people and AI depends on assigning responsibility as execution becomes easier.
